Single transistor vs half H-bridge vs full H-bridge
---------------------------------------------------

- A single transistor in low-side or high-side configuration is the 
  simplest way to drive a motor, just remember the clamping diode across 
  the motor.

- The advantage of a H-bridge (half or full) as opposed to a single 
  transistor, is that it can brake. That's because a H-bridge can source 
  and sink current and thus it can short-circuit the motor. When the 
  motor is free spinning, it acts as a generator and shorting it, will 
  cause it to brake because the generated energy gets consumed.

- A full H-bridge as opposed to a half H-bridge can also control the 
  motor direction.


Full H-bridge modes
-------------------

1. Drive/Coast mode (uses 3 pins)

   The two H-bridge inputs determine the direction and the ENABLE pin is 
   driven by a PWM signal. It is possible to brake the motor by setting 
   the two inputs to the same state (either LOW or HIGH) while having 
   the ENABLE pin HIGH.

2. Sign-Magnitude mode (uses 2 pins)

   Only the two H-bridge inputs are used, one is set to either HIGH or 
   LOW for the direction and the other is connected to a PWM signal for 
   the speed. If there is an ENABLE pin, it is tied HIGH.

3. Lock Anti-phase mode (uses 1 or 2 pins)

   This mode drives each H-bridge input with a separate PWM signal, one 
   of which is 180° out of phase with the other. If there is an ENABLE 
   pin, it is tied HIGH. If an external inverter is available, a single 
   PWM signal can be used. If the PWM duty cycle is less than 50%, the 
   motor turns in one direction with maximum speed at 0% duty cycle. 
   Above 50% duty cycle, the speed increases in the other direction with 
   the maximum speed at 100% duty cycle.

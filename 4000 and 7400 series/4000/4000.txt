For the vast majority of projects, there is no functional difference 
between a chip starting with CD, HEF, MC1, HCF or TC. If the chips are 
of the same type (like 4017B), they perform the exact same logic function 
and have the exact same pinout. The difference entirely comes down to 
the history of who originally designed them, that's their silicon 
layouts are different. This leads to a few extremely minor differences, 
but unless you are working on a high-speed, hyper-sensitive circuit 
pushing the extreme outer limits of the datasheet, you can safely use 
them interchangeably.


Common Types
------------

Buffer:
4050   hex (high current sinking, unusual supply pinout, 
            replaces the problematic 4010)

NOT:
4007   3 raw complementary pairs
4049   hex (high current sinking, unusual supply pinout, 
            replaces the problematic 4009)
4069   hex
40106  hex (schmitt trigger inputs)

Logic gates:
4001   quad NOR
4011   quad NAND
4070   quad XOR (replaces the problematic 4030)
4071   quad OR
4081   quad AND

Counters and Dividers:
4017   decade counter that sequentially activates Q0 through Q9
4029   up/down counter with BCD or binary output
4020   14-stage ripple counter (like the 4060, but without oscillator)
4024   7-stage ripple counter
4040   12-stage ripple counter
Notes:
- The 4017 and 4029 are synchronous counters that change their outputs 
  simultaneously.
- The 4020/4024/4040 are asynchronous counters that do not change 
  their outputs simultaneously.

Flip-flop:
4013   dual D-type (edge triggered, async. set/reset)
4027   dual JK-type (edge triggered, async. set/reset)

Shift register:
4094   8-bit serial-in to parallel-out (async. out latch, 3-state output)
4021   8-Bit parallel-in to serial-out (async. parallel load)

Digital Multiplexers / Demultiplexers:
They exist in the 4000-series, but are not mentioned here because the analog 
multiplexer stole their job for low-speed tasks, the 7400-series stole their 
job for high-speed tasks, and shift registers stole their job for MCU I/O 
expansion.

Analog switch and Analog Mux/Demux:
4066   quad bilateral switch (use a 4053 if Vᴇᴇ is needed)
4051   8-channel mux (Vᴇᴇ pin to set analog negative voltage)
4052   dual 4-channel mux (Vᴇᴇ pin to set analog negative voltage)
4053   triple 2-channel mux (Vᴇᴇ pin to set analog negative voltage)
4067   16-channel mux

Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) and Oscillator/Timer:
4046   voltage controlled oscillator (VCO) and two phase comparators
4047   monostable / astable multivibrator (flawless 50% duty-cycle)
4060   14-stage ripple counter with oscillator (often used as timer)


Suffix and Voltage
------------------

Pay close attention to the suffixes at the end of the part number, as 
these actually affect how the chip behaves:
- "A" (Unbuffered and older): this is the original, older 1970s spec. 
  The A-series chips are unbuffered and usually only rated for a maximum 
  of 15V. 
- "B" (Buffered): this is the modern standard. The outputs are buffered, 
  meaning they provide stronger, more consistent logic levels. 
  This series supports a wider voltage range of 3V to 18V. 
- "UB" (Unbuffered): sometimes single-stage chips are still needed 
  because of the shorter propagation delays, to do simple oscillators 
  or for linear analog operations. So, the manufacturers created the 
  "UB" line which uses the single-stage design of the A-series but with 
  the new silicon fabrication process of the B-series. This means that 
  the UB-series also supports the wider voltage range of 3V to 18V. 
  Only NANDs, NORs, inverters and the 4007 are available as "UB" chips.


Speed
-----

Gate propagation time: ~250ns  @ 3.3V
                       ~120ns  @ 5V
                       ~40ns   @ 15V

Typical max frequency: ~1.5MHz @ 3.3V
                       ~3.5MHz @ 5V
                       ~12MHz  @ 15V

Note: these figures apply to the A-series and the B-series. The UB-series 
is twice as fast, although only when driving not too heavy loads.


Current
-------

The "A" and "UB" series are highly asymmetrical. Their internal P-ch 
MOSFETs (which connect the outputs to VDD) are physically weaker than 
their N-ch MOSFETs (which connect the outputs to Ground). This means that 
their current sinking capability is twice as high as their sourcing 
capability. Note that the current sinking capability of the UB-series is 
slightly inferior to the current capability of the B-series.

The "B" series chips are symmetrical (except for the 4050B, 40107B and 
4511B), they can source and sink the exact same amount of current:
~0.2mA @ 3.3V
~1.0mA @ 5V
~6.8mA @ 15V

The 4049UB/4050B chips were designed to pull TTL inputs low. This means 
they can sink around 5 times more current than they can source. 
Hint: we can drive a LED by connecting its cathode to a 4049UB/4050B output.

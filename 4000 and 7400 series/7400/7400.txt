For the vast majority of projects, there is no functional difference 
between a chip starting with SN74, CD74, DM74, MC74 or HD74. If 
the chips are of the same type (like 74HC00), they perform the exact 
same logic function and have the exact same pinout. The difference 
entirely comes down to the history of who originally designed them, 
that's their silicon layouts are different. This leads to a few 
extremely minor differences, but unless you are working on a high-speed, 
hyper-sensitive circuit pushing the extreme outer limits of the 
datasheet, you can safely use them interchangeably.


Common Types
------------

Buffer:
74125  quad (3-state output)
74244  octal buffers and line drivers with 3-state outputs
74245  octal bus transceiver with 3-state outputs

NOT:
7404   hex
7405   hex (open collector / open drain)
7414   hex (schmitt trigger inputs)

Logic gates:
7400   quad NAND
7402   quad NOR (unusual gate layout)
7408   quad AND
7432   quad OR
7486   quad XOR

Counters and Dividers:
744017 decade counter that sequentially activates Q0 through Q9
7490   divide-by-two + divide-by-five ripple counter (async. reset)
7493   divide-by-two + divide-by-eight ripple counter (async. reset)
74160  up counter with BCD output 0..9 (sync. preset & async. reset)
74161  up counter with binary output 0..15 (sync. preset & async. reset)
74163  up counter with binary output 0..15 (sync. preset & sync. reset)
74190  up/down counter with BCD output 0..9 (async. preset)
74191  up/down counter with binary output 0..15 (async. preset)
Attention:
- The 7490/7493 use ripple-clocking, therefore, their outputs do not 
  change simultaneously.
- The 7490/7493 have an unusual supply pinout!
- The BCD counters are becoming increasingly rare these days. However, we 
  can create decade counters by using a binary counter with an AND gate:
  sync. reset  = QD AND QA
  async. reset = QD AND QB

Flip-flop:
7473   dual JK-type (master-slave updating output on falling edge), 
       has only an async. reset
7474   dual D-type (edge triggered)
7476   dual JK-type (master-slave updating output on falling edge)
74112  dual JK-type (negative edge triggered) - better than 7473/7476
74373  octal D-type (transparent latch, 3-state output)
74573  octal D-type (transparent latch, 3-state output),
       a 74373 with a nice pinout
74374  octal D-type (edge triggered, 3-state output)
74574  octal D-type (edge triggered, 3-state output),
       a 74374 with a nice pinout
Attention:
- The original 7473/7476 are master-slave JK flip-flops, which means 
  that when the clock goes HIGH, the master section opens and 
  continuously monitors the J and K inputs. If J goes HIGH at any point 
  while CLK is HIGH, the master latch locks in that '1', if J returns 
  LOW before CLK goes LOW, the master latch does not change, it retains 
  the previously captured '1'. The stored state is transferred to the 
  slave outputs on the falling edge.
- The 7473/7476 have an unusual supply pinout!

Shift register:
74595  8-bit serial-in to parallel-out (sync. out latch, 3-state output)
74165  8-Bit parallel-in to serial-out (async. parallel load)

Digital Multiplexer:
74157  quad 2 to 1
74153  dual 4 to 1
74151  8 to 1

Digital Demultiplexer:
74139  dual 1 to 4
74138  1 to 8
74154  1 to 16 (instead of this we can use two 74138)

Analog switch and Analog Mux/Demux:
744316 quad bilateral switch (Vᴇᴇ pin to set analog negative voltage)
744051 8-channel mux (Vᴇᴇ pin to set analog negative voltage)
744052 dual 4-channel mux (Vᴇᴇ pin to set analog negative voltage)
744053 triple 2-channel mux (Vᴇᴇ pin to set analog negative voltage)
744067 16-channel mux


Families and Levels
-------------------

5V only:
- The 74LSxx family works with 5V TTL levels.
- The 74HCTxx and 74AHCTxx families work with 5V TTL input levels and 
  5V CMOS output levels.

Variable voltage:
- The 74HCxx family works with 2V-6V CMOS levels. Over-voltages at the 
  inputs are not allowed because of the input clamping diodes to Vcc. 
- The 74AHCxx family works with 2V-5.5V CMOS levels and the inputs 
  are over-voltage tolerant up to 5.5V, even if the chip is running at 
  a lower voltage.
- The 74LVxx family works with 1V-5.5V CMOS levels. Over-voltages at 
  the inputs are not allowed because of the input clamping diodes to Vcc.
- The 74LVCxx family works with 1.65V-3.6V CMOS levels and the inputs 
  are over-voltage tolerant up to 5.5V.
  When operated at 3.3V, the input thresholds are deliberately set to be 
  TTL-compatible.

Modernization limitations:
The 4000-series imports of the 4017, 4046, 4047, 4060, 4066, 4051, 4052, 4053 
and 4067 exist as 74HCxx/74HCTxx variants. Many of them exist also as 74LVxx 
variants, but only the 4066 is available as 74AHC4066 and as 74LVC4066.

Little logic:
There are small SMD chips available for many modern families. You recognize 
them by a 1G or a 2G between the family name and the part number. A 1G means 
single gate or 5-pin/6-pin package and a 2G means dual gate or 8-pin package: 
74xx1G14, 74xx1G04, 74xx1G125, 74xx1G00, 74xx1G02, 74xx1G08, 74xx1G32, 
74xx1G86, 74xx1G74, 74xx1G66 (single analog switch, name comes from the 4066), 
74xx2G53 (single 2:1 analog mux with inhibit pin, name comes from the 4053), 
74xx1G3157 (single 2:1 analog mux without inhibit pin, name is composed of 
the 3 which indicates analog pass-through switch and the 157 which comes from 
the 74157).


Speed
-----

Gate propagation time: ~10ns   74LSxx
                       ~10ns   74HCxx,  74HCTxx
                       ~5ns    74AHCxx, 74AHCTxx
                       ~8ns    74LVxx
                       ~4ns    74LVCxx

Typical max frequency: ~33MHz  74LSxx
                       ~60MHz  74HCxx,  74HCTxx
                       ~150MHz 74AHCxx, 74AHCTxx
                       ~80MHz  74LVxx
                       ~160MHz 74LVCxx


Current
-------

74LSxx can sink 8mA and source only 0.4mA
74HCxx, 74HCTxx can sink/source around 4mA
74AHCxx, 74AHCTxx can sink/source around 8mA
74LVxx can sink/source around 12mA
74LVCxx can sink/source around 24mA
